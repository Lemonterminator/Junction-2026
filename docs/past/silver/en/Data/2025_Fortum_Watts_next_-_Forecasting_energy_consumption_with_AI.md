## Challenge Description

Forecasting the future of energy

The Story:
It’s a winter evening in Finland. As temperatures plunge, heaters hum to life and electricity demand surges. For energy companies, anticipating these consumption spikes is mission-critical – a poor forecast can leave costly excess or cause scramble for power at premium rates. Green energy transition, geopolitical situation, and changing customer habits make usage patterns even harder to predict. One sudden cold front or a popular live TV event can send consumption off the charts. In short, forecasting energy demand is no simple task – yet cracking this puzzle holds the key to a more reliable, efficient, and green energy future.

We challenge you to build an AI-powered model that forecasts future energy consumption with unprecedented accuracy. You will be given anonymized, normalized time-series data of energy usage across several customer segments/products (real historical data scaled down for confidentiality). Your mission is to develop a forecasting solution that learns the patterns in this data and predicts consumption for upcoming periods (e.g. the next day or week) under varying conditions. To succeed, you’ll need to combine sophisticated modeling techniques with creative feature engineering. Leverage any external open data sources that could improve predictions – think weather forecasts, temperature and daylight info, public holidays or events, economic indicators, etc. The goal is to capture the drivers of energy use: can your model “see” that a Monday snowstorm after a mild weekend will spike demand? Or that longer daylight hours will subtly curb evening consumption? This is a difficult, real-world forecasting problem designed for the brightest teams – expect to push the limits of your skills and ingenuity.

## Challenge Insights

Accurate consumption forecasts are more than a tech demo – they can stabilize the energy grid and save money. Even a small improvement in prediction accuracy can translate into significant cost savings in energy trading and procurement. Better forecasts mean power plants optimize production, less standby reserve is wasted, and fewer surprises in balancing supply and demand. In the bigger picture, improved forecasting supports the transition to cleaner energy by helping integrate renewables (whose output can be intermittent) more smoothly into the grid. Ultimately, cracking this challenge could reduce waste, lower costs, and cut emissions, making energy more affordable and sustainable for everyone. This is your chance to contribute to that future in a tangible way.
