## 1. Track Definition & Reorganization Criteria

**Data Track** focuses on massive structured/unstructured data engineering, time-series forecasting, geospatial analysis, real-time streaming data indexing, multi-source open data fusion, industrial/urban digital twin data foundations, and industry data value monetization.

### Reorganization Criteria
This catalog comprehensively covers all challenge topics within the `AI & Data` track centered on data analytics, predictive modeling, feature engineering, and visualization, and systematically consolidates **57 high-value data challenges** from `Sustainability`, `Urban & Mobility`, `Entertainment`, `Security`, `Education`, `Health`, `IoT & Hardware`, and `Other`, organized across six business and technical domains:

1. **Energy, Power Grid & Industrial Time-Series Forecasting and Fault Diagnosis**: e.g., Fortum's high-precision time-series forecasting of electricity consumption across customer segments; Valmet x HSY's wastewater network flow and electricity price forecasting; Digital BCG's energy crisis data analysis; Outotec's acoustic/vibration-based fault prediction for metallurgical plants; Valmet's micron-level geometric measurement analysis of paper machine rollers; Oras's building water usage prediction from JSON data; Vaisala's meteorological data fusion; Denso's EV charging demand forecasting and anomaly detection.

2. **Retail Supply Chain, Logistics Scheduling & Sustainable Consumption Data Mining**: e.g., Relex's real-time POS and visual data optimization for zero-waste fresh food retail; Valio Aimo's pick-face out-of-stock probability prediction across 20,000+ food service customers; K-Group and CGI & Kesko's shopping basket transaction data mining for supermarkets; QOCO's air cargo capacity and end-to-end baggage tracking data; Fleetboard's real-time heavy truck telemetry data; Cronvall's industrial procurement carbon emission data; Outotec's material environmental footprint data; Columbia Road's e-commerce anti-mis-purchase data analysis.

3. **Geospatial Analysis, Urban Big Data & Pedestrian Flow Situational Awareness**: e.g., McKinsey's geospatial analysis and site selection scoring modeling; Microsoft Finland's multi-source open data science; Business Finland's analysis of 1.7 billion rows of Helsinki urban dynamics and tourist trajectory data; Telia's telecom crowd mobility big data; Aalto's real-time WiFi pedestrian flow prediction for campus and large events; STARA's Helsinki municipal maintenance and street renovation big data; Tampere's urban traffic guidance data; Visit Finland's hourly visitor flow CSV forecasting for national parks; Aalto's open space and satellite remote sensing data.

4. **Building Digital Twins, Indoor mmWave Radar/Sensor Networks & 3D Engineering Data**: e.g., KONE x Granlund's trillion-scale building MEP asset data collection and digital twin; Helvar & VTT's 60GHz/24GHz mmWave radar and RGB skeletal multi-modal indoor perception; Helvar's multi-source building data fusion and automatic sensor topology localization; Trimble's 3D BIM engineering data; Peikko's IFC building metadata mining; Stara's municipal building renovation project data governance.

5. **Real-Time Data Indexing, Enterprise Analytics Platforms, Financial Regulation & Open Data Economy**: e.g., BNB Chain's real-time on-chain data indexing protocol; SAS Viya and CI 360 enterprise-grade analytics platforms; Suomen Pankki's 55GB financial regulatory data analysis based on Medallion architecture (Bronze/Silver/Gold); Espoo's startup–large enterprise matching algorithm and automated regional open data analysis; Sitra's personal data sovereignty; TX Streamr's Web3 data union monetization; Gravity Team's multi-source signal and sentiment-based pricing analysis for crypto markets.

6. **Healthcare, Biometrics, Cybersecurity & Game Behavior Data Science**: e.g., CGI & City of Turku's precision recommendation for preventing social isolation among elderly living alone; Varian's radiotherapy treatment plan database analysis; Sitra's Azure data lake health and sensor data analysis; Huawei's wearable sensor-based motion and emotion modeling; Pixieray's 12-channel infrared eye-tracking and behavior correlation analysis for smart glasses; Pfizer x Aava's migraine onset biosignal prediction; Ericsson's core network signaling anomaly detection and threat intelligence data pipeline; D-Fence's phishing email classification; Kamu and Epic Games' real-time game telemetry data mining; Veikkaus's sports statistics data modeling.

### 1. [2025] Fortum — Watt's next? - Forecasting energy consumption with AI

- **File**: [2025_Fortum_Watts_next_-_Forecasting_energy_consumption_with_AI.md](./2025_Fortum_Watts_next_-_Forecasting_energy_consumption_with_AI.md)
- **Original Track**: `AI & Data` | **Sub-theme**: I. Energy, Grid & Industrial Time-Series Forecasting and Fault Diagnosis (Energy, Grid & Industrial Time-Series Forecasting)
- **Core Theme**: High-precision time-series forecasting of multi-customer-segment electricity load integrating external features such as weather, solar irradiance, holidays, and social events
- **Classification Rationale**: Provides desensitized real historical electricity consumption time-series data, requiring feature engineering and future daily/weekly load forecasting with a 100% accuracy weight combined with external open data sources—an extremely classic Data Science time-series forecasting challenge.

#### Description

Forecasting the future of energy

The Story:
It’s a winter evening in Finland. As temperatures plunge, heaters hum to life and electricity demand surges. For energy companies, anticipating these consumption spikes is mission-critical – a poor forecast can leave costly excess or cause scramble for power at premium rates. Green energy transition, geopolitical situation, and changing customer habits make usage patterns even harder to predict. One sudden cold front or a popular live TV event can send consumption off the charts. In short, forecasting energy demand is no simple task – yet cracking this puzzle holds the key to a more reliable, efficient, and green energy future.

We challenge you to build an AI-powered model that forecasts future energy consumption with unprecedented accuracy. You will be given anonymized, normalized time-series data of energy usage across several customer segments/products (real historical data scaled down for confidentiality). Your mission is to develop a forecasting solution that learns the patterns in this data and predicts consumption for upcoming periods (e.g. the next day or week) under varying conditions. To succeed, you’ll need to combine sophisticated modeling techniques with creative feature engineering. Leverage any external open data sources that could improve predictions – think weather forecasts, temperature and daylight info, public holidays or events, economic indicators, etc. The goal is to capture the drivers of energy use: can your model “see” that a Monday snowstorm after a mild weekend will spike demand? Or that longer daylight hours will subtly curb evening consumption? This is a difficult, real-world forecasting problem designed for the brightest teams – expect to push the limits of your skills and ingenuity.

#### Resources

We will provide the core consumption dataset (with timestamps, values and segment identifiers) to get you started. How you enrich it is up to you: crawl open APIs for weather data, incorporate calendar info, or even scrape relevant news – be bold in connecting the dots. Your solution can utilize any methods (statistical models, machine learning, deep learning, hybrid approaches) and any tools or libraries. While the primary focus is the accuracy of your forecasts, you should also consider aspects like model robustness (handling noisy data or anomalies) and explainability (can you identify which factors drive the predictions?).

    Fortum provides:
Access to normalized energy consumption data

Mentors from Fortum’s data science, energy systems, and operations teams

Technical office hours for advice on energy domain context and AI feasibility
Extra: Fortum has run multiple innovation projects and hackathon collaborations in the past, which means your solutions can have a real pathway to pilots and production if they show strong potential.

#### Prizes

€3,000 cash prize pool divided among the top teams, plus Fortum-branded gear and an opportunity for the winning team to pitch their solution to Fortum’s innovation leadership.

Internship opportunities at Fortum’s AI Solutions team for best 3 teams

### 2. [2025] Valmet x HSY — Intelligent Flow Optimization

- **File**: [2025_Valmet_x_HSY_Intelligent_Flow_Optimization.md](./2025_Valmet_x_HSY_Intelligent_Flow_Optimization.md)
- **Original Track**: `Sustainability` | **Sub-theme**: I. Energy, Grid & Industrial Time-Series Forecasting and Fault Diagnosis (Energy, Grid & Industrial Time-Series Forecasting)
- **Core Theme**: Hydraulic flow regime modeling for pumping stations based on historical wastewater inflow, storm forecasts, and 15-minute-level dynamic electricity price data
- **Classification Rationale**: From the Sustainability track, provides HSY's decades-long real wastewater and stormwater inflow historical dataset, requiring multi-step forecasting and optimization combined with real-time fluctuating electricity prices and pipe network capacity constraints.

#### Description

Build a multi-agent AI that autonomously minimizes wastewater pumping energy—one of the most critical processes in modern infrastructure—by forecasting inflow, power prices, and flow dynamics in real time.

Every city relies on invisible systems that keep life running—and wastewater management is one of the most critical. Pumps operate nonstop, consuming massive amounts of energy while facing the chaos of inflow, storms, and fluctuating electricity prices. What if these networks could think for themselves? With multi-agent AI, Valmet and HSY invite you to transform static infrastructure into a living system that predicts, adapts, and optimizes wastewater flow in real time.

Wastewater networks operate 24/7 and cannot stop — yet they hide untapped potential for intelligent optimization. Each pumping station must balance constant flow, limited storage, and fluctuating electricity prices while staying resilient to stormwater surges. Traditional automation follows fixed rules and ignores real-time changes and forecasts. Your task is to build a multi-agent AI system where autonomous agents plan pumping according to daily energy prices, coordinate flow between stations, and find the most energy-efficient pumping strategy without violating safety or process constraints. The goal: make one of society’s most critical infrastructure systems self-optimizing.

#### Resources

Participants will have access to HSY’s historical wastewater and inflow datasets—real operational data spanning years of environmental variation. System communication and control will be implemented through the OPC UA protocol, the industrial standard for secure, real-time data exchange. Mentors from Valmet, HSY, and Aalto University will provide hands-on guidance in areas such as process automation, multi-agent architectures, and in future solutions can be tested with digital twin simulation. Teams are free to use any AI framework of their choice—from reinforcement learning environments to optimization libraries or custom-built agent systems.
